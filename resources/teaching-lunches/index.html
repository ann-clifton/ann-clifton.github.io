teaching lunches

Topics, discussion questions, and resources from the Teaching Lunch series.

Teaching Lunches are lunchtime conversations about teaching for the Mathematics & Statistics faculty at Louisiana Tech. We meet in the AXIOM Lab (Bogard 319). Below are summaries of the topics along with any resources shared.

Assessments: more than exams and homework

Tuesday, November 17, 2026

How do we gauge student understanding in the age of AI? We looked at assignments students engage with meaningfully and can’t simply hand to a computer: one-minute activities, oral exams, group work, exit tickets, and more.

Discussion questions

  • What assessments have you used that still work well, and which ones have stopped working?
  • What would it take to try something like an oral exam or exit ticket in your course?

Resources

  • Plickers — Free cards to print out for each student to use to respond to multiple choice polling questions. There is a corresponding app that you use to scan the responses and see results in real time. I have used these in my calculus classes and they work really well.
  • GoodQuestions — Calculus polling questions (great to use with Plickers!) created by Cornell. A lot of these are really conceptual and can lead to great class discussions.
  • Journal of Inquiry-Based Learning in Mathematics — Peer-reviewed course notes using an inquiry-based learning (IBL) approach. In an IBL course, the professor acts as a facilitator while the students actively work through the material. There may be mini-lectures, but most of the class time is dedicated to groups working through the material via guided notes and handouts.
  • Active Calculus — A popular inquiry-based calculus text that is a free open resource. I have used some of the activities in my calculus courses here.
  • Center for Grading Reform — Resources on alternative grading practices.

AI and teaching

Tuesday, October 27, 2026

How AI can help us and our students when used responsibly, and why math still matters as these tools get better at solving problems.

Discussion questions

  • Are you using AI yourself, for things like lesson or material prep or note generation? How is it going?
  • How can we make sure our students use these tools responsibly?

Resources

  • Statement on AI in Higher Education — Mathematical Association of America.
  • AI resources for the mathematics community — The American Mathematical Society’s collection of readings, tools, and guidance on AI and mathematics.
  • Podcast (47 min) — Steven Strogatz on On Point, about AI’s recent major advances in pure math and what they could mean for the future.
  • Video (40 min) — 3Blue1Brown, “There’s never been a better time to go into math.”
  • Blog post — Francis Su, “The Enduring Value of Math, in an Age of AI.” What he told his students on the first day of class.
