Teaching Lunches are lunchtime conversations about teaching for the Mathematics & Statistics faculty at Louisiana Tech. We meet in the [AXIOM Lab]({{ '/axiom-lab/' | relative_url }}) (Bogard 319). Below are summaries of the topics along with any resources shared.

## Assessments: more than exams and homework

*Tuesday, November 17, 2026*

How do we gauge student understanding in the age of AI? We looked at assignments students engage with meaningfully and can't simply hand to a computer: one-minute activities, oral exams, group work, exit tickets, and more.

**Discussion questions**

- What assessments have you used that still work well, and which ones have stopped working?
- What would it take to try something like an oral exam or exit ticket in your course?

**Resources**

- [Plickers](https://help.plickers.com/hc/en-us/articles/360009395854-What-is-Plickers) — Free cards to print out for each student to use to respond to multiple choice polling questions. There is a corresponding app that you use to scan the responses and see results in real time. I have used these in my calculus classes and they work really well.
- [GoodQuestions](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~GoodQuestions/materials.html) — Calculus polling questions (great to use with Plickers!) created by Cornell. A lot of these are really conceptual and can lead to great class discussions.
- [Journal of Inquiry-Based Learning in Mathematics](https://jiblm.org/guides/index.php?category=jiblmjournal) — Peer-reviewed course notes using an inquiry-based learning (IBL) approach. In an IBL course, the professor acts as a facilitator while the students actively work through the material. There may be mini-lectures, but most of the class time is dedicated to groups working through the material via guided notes and handouts.
- [Active Calculus](https://activecalculus.org/acs/) — A popular inquiry-based calculus text that is a free open resource. I have used some of the activities in my calculus courses here.
- [Center for Grading Reform](https://www.centerforgradingreform.org/resources/) — Resources on alternative grading practices.

## AI and teaching

*Tuesday, October 27, 2026*

How AI can help us and our students when used responsibly, and why math still matters as these tools get better at solving problems.

**Discussion questions**

- Are you using AI yourself, for things like lesson or material prep or note generation? How is it going?
- How can we make sure our students use these tools responsibly?

**Resources**

- [Statement on AI in Higher Education](https://maa.org/resource/statement-on-ai-in-higher-education/) — Mathematical Association of America.
- [AI resources for the mathematics community](https://www.ams.org/ai/ai) — The American Mathematical Society's collection of readings, tools, and guidance on AI and mathematics.
- [Podcast (47 min)](https://www.wbur.org/onpoint/2026/10/13/ai-steamrolling-math-solve) — Steven Strogatz on On Point, about AI's recent major advances in pure math and what they could mean for the future.
- [Video (40 min)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LKrwhjOI8Nc) — 3Blue1Brown, "There's never been a better time to go into math."
- [Blog post](https://francissu.substack.com/p/the-enduring-value-of-math-in-an) — Francis Su, "The Enduring Value of Math, in an Age of AI." What he told his students on the first day of class.
